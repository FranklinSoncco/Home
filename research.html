Thesis Research

Final degree project — Mechatronics Engineering

Team of 3 · PFC II

6-DoF Active Upper-Limb Exoskeleton with Impedance Control and EMG Feedback for Muscular Fatigue Reduction in Manual Coffee Harvesting

Manual coffee harvesting demands repetitive shoulder-height movements and produces musculoskeletal loads that are significant for agricultural workers. Conventional mechanisation is impractical on the small plots and mountainous terrain typical of Peruvian coffee production. This project developed and evaluated an active upper-limb exoskeleton built around a lightweight mechatronic architecture with motion sensing and sEMG signals.

The methodology integrated conceptual selection under VDI 2225 / VDI 2206, kinematic and dynamic modelling, mechanical design in carbon-fibre-reinforced PETG, finite-element validation, joint-level control implementation and experimental evaluation of muscular load.

Results

Structural validation
Finite-element analysis confirmed mechanical feasibility for the anticipated operating loads.
Control simulation
PD control with gravity compensation showed stable convergence with zero steady-state error; holding torques of 3 Nm (shoulder flexion), 0.0 Nm (shoulder abduction) and 0.2 Nm (elbow flexion).
Impedance test
The prototype operated for 600 s at 100 Hz, delivering assistance modulated by movement phase and tracking the active joints through harvesting cycles.
Perceived exertion
Borg scale fell from 17 (without exoskeleton) to 10, and finally to 7 in the movement-intention condition.
Muscular load (sEMG)
Posterior deltoid activation fell 28% on average, p95 peak 51% and accumulated load (OMSum) 77%. Triceps reduced the same metrics by 19%, 28% and 65%. No progressive myoelectric fatigue was detected.

Fatigue and muscular-load evaluation was conducted as a preliminary single-subject case study (n = 1) across three conditions. Population-level validation requires a larger sample and per-session pairing of CVM measurements.
